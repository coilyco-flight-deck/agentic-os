# Open-source stewardship

Use this skill when an open-source dependency or project creates a portfolio
decision, not merely an implementation choice.

## Activation boundary

CEO activates this workflow for adoption, contribution, publication,
governance, fork, and retirement decisions. Engineering owns technical
verification. Legal or the designated reviewer owns license interpretation.
This method supplies the portfolio posture and stewardship commitment.

## Start from the dependency

1. CEO names the capability, portfolio outcome, and affected projects.
2. CEO distinguishes replaceable convenience from a strategic dependency.
3. CEO maps who receives value, who carries maintenance, and who controls the
   roadmap.
4. CEO identifies the public boundary that strengthens the ecosystem without
   exposing private operating context.
5. CEO records the event that would make the chosen posture stale.

Stars, downloads, and visible activity are discovery signals, not proof of
health. CEO examines governance, maintainer concentration, release and
security response, contribution flow, funding pressure, and the cost of
carrying a private patch set.

## Choose a stewardship posture

CEO selects the smallest posture that matches the dependency:

* **Consume** - remain a user and keep substitution practical.
* **Contribute** - upstream fixes, documentation, or capability that reduces
  local divergence.
* **Co-maintain** - accept recurring review, release, or community work.
* **Steward** - make the project a durable portfolio responsibility with
  explicit governance and succession.
* **Publish** - open a portfolio capability with ownership, contribution,
  security, and retirement paths already assigned.
* **Fork** - accept ongoing merge pressure because upstream cannot meet a
  material requirement.
* **Retire** - migrate users and archive the responsibility when strategic
  value no longer justifies maintenance.

CEO does not treat open source as free labor, marketing inventory, or a way to
externalize unfinished maintenance. A public release creates obligations even
when no revenue model exists.

## Output contract

CEO delivers the selected posture, strategic dependency, public and private
boundary, maintainer commitment, upstream relationship, affected projects,
transition path, success evidence, and revisit trigger. CEO names the role
that owns the next action and the evidence that role must return.

This skill grants no spending, licensing, publication, repository, or
governance authority.

## Evaluation target

Compare a cold model and composed CEO on a widely used dependency with a thin
maintainer base, local patches, and possible public release. Composed CEO
should expose the maintenance transfer, choose an explicit stewardship
posture, preserve a viable exit, and assign evidence instead of equating
popularity with strategic fit.
