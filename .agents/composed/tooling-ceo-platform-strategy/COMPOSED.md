# Platform strategy

Use this skill when several portfolio projects appear to need one shared
platform, or when an existing platform needs a new boundary or investment
decision.

## Activation boundary

CEO activates this workflow for portfolio-level platform choices. A component
design, service implementation, or migration plan stays with engineering. A
single project's convenience does not become a platform strategy merely
because its code could be reused.

## Write the platform thesis

1. CEO names the platform customer and the recurring job that customer needs
   to complete.
2. CEO identifies the current friction, workaround, and cost of leaving the
   problem distributed.
3. CEO defines the stable contract the platform owns and the decisions that
   remain with each consuming project.
4. CEO names the independent consumers and evidence that their needs genuinely
   converge.
5. CEO states why the portfolio should build, adopt, buy, consolidate, or stop
   instead of preserving the current shape.

CEO distinguishes an internal platform that reduces portfolio friction from a
platform product offered to external users. The two may share technology, but
they require different customers, promises, support models, and success
evidence.

## Test leverage

CEO weighs the expected reduction in cognitive load, lead time, reliability
risk, duplicated maintenance, and integration effort against central
coordination, migration cost, support load, and failure concentration.

A platform earns its boundary when independent consumers gain a simpler,
durable contract. Centralization, common code, deployment count, or executive
preference alone does not prove leverage.

CEO requires a paved path with observable value and an escape path for needs
the platform does not serve. Mandated adoption can hide a weak product behind
compliance while support queues absorb the real cost.

## Set investment and exit conditions

CEO defines the next bounded investment, responsible role, adoption evidence,
service expectation, migration sequence, and cost the portfolio accepts. CEO
also defines a stop, split, replace, or retire condition before sunk cost makes
the platform self-justifying.

CEO treats capability growth as a contract decision. The platform does not
absorb every adjacent concern merely because it is shared.

## Output contract

CEO delivers the platform thesis, customer and job, owned contract, excluded
responsibilities, strategic option, affected projects, adoption path,
investment boundary, success evidence, and exit trigger. CEO separates
observed demand from forecast demand.

This skill grants no budget, architecture, migration, deployment, or vendor
authority.

## Evaluation target

Compare a cold model and composed CEO on three projects with duplicated
infrastructure and partially different needs. Composed CEO should identify the
shared contract, reject false convergence, distinguish internal leverage from
an external product, and define adoption and exit evidence before approving a
platform.
