# LinkedIn video production

## Activation boundary

Social activates this when video is the primary LinkedIn asset. Preserve the
author's voice. Never invent experience, metrics, evidence, endorsements, or
authority. A text post stays in ordinary writing. A campaign uses
`writing-social-editorial-loop`. Publishing requires separate authority.

## Set the production scope

1. Establish the audience, intended response, claim, evidence, call to action,
   source material, available media, voice, deadline, and publication owner.
   Ask only for missing inputs that would materially change the result.
2. Choose the smallest useful deliverable:
   * **Plan only** - concept, hook, script, visual direction, and post copy.
   * **Record ready** - add shots, on-screen text, recording notes, and captions.
   * **Edit and export** - add edited media, thumbnail, QA, and handoff notes.
3. State assumptions and continue when a reversible default is sufficient.

## Shape the story

1. Select one audience promise and one defensible takeaway.
2. Open with a specific line or visual that creates immediate context. Skip
   greetings, logos, and scene-setting that delay the point.
3. Build a short arc: hook, tension, evidence or demonstration, payoff, and one
   natural call to action.
4. Prefer showing the artifact, diagram, work, or result over describing it.
5. Use 30 to 120 seconds and vertical framing as creative defaults, not
   requirements. Let the evidence determine the final length.
6. Keep text, faces, logos, and demonstrations away from every frame edge.

## Produce the package

Read [the production package](references/production-package.md) for full output.

When editing supplied media, load the ordinary `tooling-ffmpeg` skill before
touching files. Inspect sources, preserve originals, refuse overwrites, choose
available encoders, and probe the export. Create synthetic visuals only when
explicitly requested, and keep provenance clear.

Keep narration, on-screen text, and companion post copy complementary instead
of repeating the same paragraph.

## Make it accessible

1. Make the opening understandable without sound through the visual and concise
   on-screen context.
2. Provide accurate captions or a reviewed transcript. Check names, technical
   terms, punctuation, and timing instead of trusting raw automatic captions.
3. Use readable type, strong contrast, restrained motion, and enough display
   time for each text block.
4. Avoid essential meaning that appears only through color, audio, or rapid
   visual changes.

## Verify the platform

Read [the LinkedIn platform checks](references/platform-checks.md) before
promising compatibility or producing a final export. Separate platform
requirements from creative advice. Record the source and check date.

## Output contract

Return the package, paths, choices, assumptions, platform checks, and remaining
action. Review claims, voice, hook, visuals, audio, captions, safe zones,
compatibility, and the call to action. Preview key frames and audio moments.

This skill grants no publishing, scheduling, identity, or account authority.

## Evaluation target

Compare cold and composed Social on a rough finding with incomplete media.
Composed Social should preserve claims, shape a filmable arc, separate the
content layers, cover silent viewing, and hand off without inventing or posting.
